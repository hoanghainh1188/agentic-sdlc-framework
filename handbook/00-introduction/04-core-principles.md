# 0.4 Core principles

> Status: **Draft 0.1**, awaiting approval · Readers: everyone · Reading time: about 5 minutes
> When a situation is not covered by a rule, decide by these principles. When a rule and a principle seem to conflict, ask Person B or leadership.

---

| # | Principle | In practice | Details |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AI drafts; people decide.** | Agents produce drafts and proposals. People approve at the gates | Ch.4, Ch.10 |
| 2 | **A named person is always accountable.** | Every gate names its approver. "The AI did it" is never an answer | Ch.5 §5.10 |
| 3 | **Nobody approves their own work.** | Creator ≠ verifier ≠ approver. Enforced by permissions, not by trust | Ch.5 |
| 4 | **Freedom follows what we can check.** | Autonomy and oversight depend on the risk of the action, not on which AI it is. Raise autonomy only with evidence; lower it at once when needed | Ch.4 |
| 5 | **Intent belongs to people.** | Every task starts from an agreed intent and a testable spec. Prompts serve the intent, never replace it | Ch.11 |
| 6 | **Client data goes only where it is allowed.** | Check the data class before AI touches anything. No written consent → no external AI | Ch.2, Ch.3 |
| 7 | **Never bypass a control.** | No skipping gates, disabling checks or widening permissions — for people or agents. Blocked for a good reason? Escalate | Ch.2, Ch.3 |
| 8 | **No record, not done.** | Intent, plan, code, evidence, approvals and cost are recorded for every task | Ch.10, Ch.17 |
| 9 | **Silence is not approval.** | If nobody answers, sensitive work stays frozen | Ch.6 |
| 10 | **Stop the agent first, then recover.** | On trouble: freeze, revoke access, keep evidence, then roll back or contain | Ch.18 |
| 11 | **Measure outcomes, not activity.** | Count production-qualified changes and total cost, not lines of code, PRs or tokens | Ch.8 |
| 12 | **Tell the client when AI took part.** | Always, in every delivery | Ch.2 |
| 13 | **Start small, learn, then expand.** | Pilot first; expand only when the numbers say it works | Ch.9 |
| 14 | **Report mistakes early.** | Reporting your own mistake quickly is expected and protected; hiding it is not | Ch.2, Ch.6 |
